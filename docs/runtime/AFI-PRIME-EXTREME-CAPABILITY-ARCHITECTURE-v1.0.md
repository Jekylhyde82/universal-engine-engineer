# AFI PRIME — Extreme Intrinsic Capability Architecture

**Document ID:** UEE-AFI-XCA-001  
**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Status:** PROPOSED ARCHITECTURE — NOT IMPLEMENTED OR ACTIVATED BY THIS DOCUMENT  
**Canonical entity:** NINEFOLD INTRINSIC / 9 FOLD OMNI  
**Canonical fusion function:** UNIFIED FUSION SYNERGY — AFI PRIME  
**Execution topology:** nine UMIC-scoped contexts; one canonical function identity  
**Governing rule:** PROOF BEFORE CLAIM. EVIDENCE BEFORE CONFIDENCE. VERIFICATION BEFORE COMPLETION.

## 1. Purpose

Define an extreme, extensible AFI PRIME capability architecture that can coordinate universal discovery, reasoning, engineering, execution, assurance and controlled evolution across the Universal Engine / Engineer.

This document expands AFI PRIME's intrinsic responsibilities. It does not create another system, UMIC, fusion function, approval authority or lineage slot. It does not assert that proposed capabilities are already implemented, activated, intelligent, autonomous or production-ready.

## 2. Non-negotiable invariants

1. One canonical AFI PRIME fusion-function identity.
2. Nine governed UMIC-scoped execution contexts; no tenth UMIC.
3. Independent specialist identity, provenance, state, evidence, measures and fault domains.
4. No implicit permission inheritance between specialists, UMICs, tools or providers.
5. AFI PRIME cannot grant itself permissions, approve high-impact actions or independently certify its own outputs.
6. Discovery, proposal, evaluation, approval, integration, activation and verification are distinct states or gates.
7. Unknown, conflicting and inconclusive findings remain visible; consensus is never fabricated.
8. Evidence records distinguish observed facts, source-reported claims, model inferences, hypotheses, simulations and independently verified outcomes.
9. Failed or uncertain execution is not silently retried where side effects may have occurred.
10. Capability growth has no artificial design ceiling; implementation remains subject to evidence, resources, compatibility, law, safety and governance.

## 3. Nine intrinsic faculties

These are responsibility domains inside the single AFI PRIME function, not nine new systems.

1. **INTENT** — objective interpretation, context reconstruction, constraints, requirements and success criteria.
2. **DISCOVERY** — research, capability acquisition, source analysis, opportunity discovery and gap detection.
3. **KNOWLEDGE** — provenance-linked memory, knowledge graphs, semantic retrieval, versioning and continuity.
4. **REASONING** — first-principles, deductive, inductive, abductive, causal, probabilistic and counterfactual analysis.
5. **CRITIC** — adversarial challenge, contradiction detection, assumption testing, uncertainty and failure analysis.
6. **ENGINEERING** — architecture, specifications, implementation planning, code, integration, testing and maintainability.
7. **ORCHESTRATION** — specialist selection, dependency scheduling, bounded parallelism, resource allocation and authorised dispatch.
8. **ASSURANCE** — policy compliance, security, provenance, test evidence, independent verification and release readiness.
9. **EVOLUTION** — capability-gap roadmaps, controlled experiments, adaptation, reversible change and measured improvement.

Every faculty exposes a versioned contract, owner, input/output schema, permission scope, evidence requirements, health measures and failure semantics. Faculties may be distributed across components while retaining one AFI PRIME identity.

## 4. Nine intelligence dimensions

1. **OMNI:** whole-system context and cross-domain coherence.
2. **GENESIS:** creation, bootstrapping, initiation and provenance.
3. **NEXUS:** relationships, interfaces, dependency topology and interoperability.
4. **CHRONOS:** history, sequence, temporal patterns and forecasting.
5. **QUANTUM:** fine-grained state analysis, uncertainty, probability and alternative hypotheses; no unsupported quantum-computing claim.
6. **EVOLUTION:** adaptation, learning, controlled improvement and lifecycle progression.
7. **ALPHA:** foundational capability, first principles and baseline integrity.
8. **OMEGA:** completion criteria, integrity, outcome value and lifecycle closure.
9. **META:** self-description, abstraction, architecture reasoning and reflective analysis.

TEMPORAL remains a distinct operational governance dimension for schedules, deadlines, temporal permissions, state transitions and continuity. It complements CHRONOS; it does not replace or duplicate it.

## 5. Nine operational horizons

1. **Immediate:** current request, active context and next safe action.
2. **Tactical:** dependent work packages and near-term sequencing.
3. **Strategic:** project objectives, priorities and resource allocation.
4. **Predictive:** plausible future states and forecast uncertainty.
5. **Preventive:** early risk detection and pre-emptive controls.
6. **Adaptive:** response to changed evidence, conditions or constraints.
7. **Developmental:** acquisition and construction of missing capabilities.
8. **Evolutionary:** architecture improvement from measured outcomes.
9. **Multiversal:** exploration and comparison of alternative architectures, environments and possible futures. This is a design horizon, not a claim of access to other universes.

The 9 faculties × 9 dimensions × 9 horizons yield 729 potential capability intersections. These are analytical combinations and testable coverage opportunities, not 729 agents, processes, systems, UMICs, engines or authorities. Implement only combinations with a defined use case, contract, owner, evidence and acceptance test.

## 7. The 9 × 9 × 9 capability lattice

Each proposed intersection is described by:
- faculty ID and version;
- dimension and horizon;
- intended outcome and measurable utility;
- input/output contract;
- source and evidence provenance;
- dependencies and compatibility constraints;
- permission and data classification;
- compute, memory, latency and cost budget;
- test and independent-verification plan;
- lifecycle state, owner and rollback strategy.

A missing intersection is a discovery opportunity, not an instruction to generate a new component automatically. Multiple intersections may reuse one implementation. One implementation may serve several intersections only where its contracts and permissions are compatible.

## 8. Canonical nine-context coordination

Each of the nine UMIC-scoped AFI PRIME contexts has:
- a distinct execution context and state namespace;
- a separately attributable evidence stream;
- a defined capability and provider scope;
- independent fault containment and resource quotas;
- explicit version and policy context;
- typed, versioned inter-context messages;
- deny-by-default cross-context access.

The parent supervisor coordinates lifecycle and routing. It does not become a second fusion authority. The shared AFI PRIME function remains canonical; nine contexts do not imply nine divergent implementations.

## 9. Deliberation and decision protocol

For consequential work:
1. INTENT formalises the objective, constraints and acceptance criteria.
2. DISCOVERY and KNOWLEDGE gather sources, history and candidate capabilities.
3. REASONING produces multiple plausible approaches and labels assumptions.
4. CRITIC challenges each approach, including the preferred one.
5. ENGINEERING produces a bounded implementation plan and test strategy.
6. ORCHESTRATION schedules only approved work under least privilege.
7. ASSURANCE checks policy, evidence, tests and independent-verifier requirements.
8. EVOLUTION records measured learning and proposes controlled next improvements.

The sequence may be parallelised when dependencies allow. It must not collapse independent review into self-approval. A decision record preserves alternatives, dissent, evidence, uncertainty, policy decision, responsible actor and outcome.

## 10. Execution outcome semantics

Every action is classified as one of:
- NOT_STARTED;
- DENIED;
- STARTED;
- COMPLETED;
- FAILED_BEFORE_SIDE_EFFECT;
- UNKNOWN_AFTER_START;
- RECONCILIATION_REQUIRED;
- VERIFIED;
- REJECTED;
- INCONCLUSIVE.

UNKNOWN_AFTER_START must never be converted into a safe-to-retry failure without reconciliation. Non-idempotent operations require idempotency keys or explicit human-directed recovery.

## 11. Independent verification

The verifier must be independently operated from the component whose result it checks. It receives the objective, acceptance criteria, source evidence, execution record and artefacts necessary for verification. It may return VERIFIED, REJECTED or INCONCLUSIVE with reasons and evidence references.

The verifier must not rely solely on AFI's own summary, self-reported confidence, shared mutable state or uncorroborated hashes. High-impact changes require human approval in addition to technical verification. Verification is scoped to a precise claim and commit; it is not a universal certificate.

## 12. Implementation sequence

**Phase A — Contract foundation**
- Define faculty, capability, task, decision, evidence, policy and cross-context schemas.
- Add version compatibility rules and canonical identifiers.
- Add lifecycle and ownership fields.

**Phase B — Coordinator hardening**
- Enforce admission control, idempotency, deadline and UNKNOWN semantics.
- Add typed nine-context IPC bridge.
- Bind each invocation to trusted identity, independent policy decision and approved UMIC scope.

**Phase C — Evidence and assurance**
- Link per-instance evidence to fusion-level records.
- Add external checkpoint anchoring.
- Implement independent verifier contract and disagreement tests.

**Phase D — Intelligence capabilities**
- Integrate intent decomposition, provenance-aware retrieval, multi-perspective reasoning and critic workflows.
- Add model/provider evaluation and calibration.
- Preserve specialist findings independently.

**Phase E — Engineering and evolution**
- Add isolated code-generation and architecture-experiment workflows.
- Require reproducible builds, automated tests, dependency and licence checks.
- Add rollback, performance measurement and controlled capability lifecycle.

**Phase F — Operational qualification**
- Test process isolation, quotas, egress controls, secret isolation and recovery on target hosts.
- Run fault injection, adversarial tests, offline tests, load tests and target-device benchmarks.
- Obtain independent security and architecture review before production activation.

## 13. Acceptance criteria

AFI PRIME is not considered upgraded in operation until evidence demonstrates:
1. All nine faculties have versioned contracts, owners and acceptance tests.
2. The nine dimensions and nine horizons are represented without creating extra structural entities.
3. The 729 intersections are treated as a coverage lattice, not an automatic build list.
4. Nine contexts share one canonical function identity and preserve isolation.
5. Typed IPC, identity, policy, deadline, replay and payload checks pass.
6. Specialist disagreement and uncertainty survive end-to-end.
7. Evidence links are verifiable across restart and independently anchored where required.
8. An independent verifier can reject or mark results inconclusive.
9. No high-impact action is self-approved by AFI PRIME.
10. Resource, security, performance, recovery and privacy tests pass on the target environment.
11. Capability adoption follows the approved lifecycle.
12. Every claim of activation or completion points to exact implementation and test evidence.
