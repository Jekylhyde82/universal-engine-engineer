**Canonical entity:** NINEFOLD INTRINSIC

**Governing rule:** PROOF BEFORE CLAIM. EVIDENCE BEFORE CONFIDENCE. VERIFICATION BEFORE COMPLETION.

## 1. Decision

All recovered, eligible capability identities are to be integrated as intrinsic specialist capabilities, responsibilities or capability domains within the one NINEFOLD INTRINSIC entity. They are not to become competing top-level intelligence systems, parallel fusion authorities or additional UMIC instances.

The one canonical AFI PRIME function remains the fusion authority. It coordinates and synthesises specialist capabilities through the nine existing UMIC-scoped instances. Each instance retains its own execution context, state namespace, evidence stream and fault-containment domain. Cross-instance exchange remains explicit, least-privilege, policy-checked and auditable.

This decision establishes the target integration contract. It does not claim that every candidate has been mapped, implemented, activated or independently verified.

## 2. Fusion semantics

Fusion does not mean flattening specialist expertise into one generic component. Each capability retains:
- its exact identity and historical provenance;
- its specialist purpose, responsibilities and limits;
- its own versioned contract, evidence and performance measures;
- the ability to disagree with another specialist, report uncertainty or refuse unsupported conclusions;
- bounded execution, permissions, resource use and fault containment;
- lifecycle state, review history and rollback path.

AFI PRIME performs cross-specialist coordination, evaluation, conflict preservation, synthesis, integration planning and traceability. It cannot grant itself permissions, approve its own high-impact actions or independently certify its own results.

## 4. Cross-cutting AFI PRIME responsibilities

The following responsibilities are fused into the existing AFI PRIME function. They do not create additional fusion authorities:

1. **Capability discovery and intake:** identify candidate capabilities and preserve source provenance.
2. **Capability identity and lineage:** maintain stable identity, version, responsibility and historical relationships.
3. **Specialist orchestration:** select eligible specialists based on task fit, evidence, permissions, quality and resource constraints.
4. **OMNIVELOCITY optimisation:** batch compatible work, parallelise independent tasks and allocate resources according to value and constraints.
5. **HYPERVELOCITY acceleration:** shorten feedback and correction loops, prioritise critical-path blockers and avoid unnecessary serial work.
6. **GENIUS BRAINSTORM:** generate and compare alternatives.
7. **GENIUS CRITIC:** challenge proposed plans and findings, surface uncertainty and failure modes.
8. **GENIUS ENGINEER:** translate approved plans into bounded implementation work.
9. **DESIGNATED DRIVER execution coordination:** dispatch only authorised actions and reconcile outcomes.
10. **Evidence and verification coordination:** preserve provenance and require independent verification appropriate to risk.
11. **Conflict and uncertainty management:** retain conflicting specialist findings until evidence or governance resolves them.
12. **Evolution management:** evaluate new capabilities without imposing an artificial discovery ceiling or bypassing approval.

These responsibilities may be implemented as modular, independently testable components. They remain one coherent AFI PRIME function identity and do not imply one monolithic process.

## 5. Ninefold execution and isolation

All integrated capabilities are assigned through the existing nine UMIC-scoped AFI PRIME instances. No tenth instance is created.

For every instance:
- execution context is separately bounded;
- mutable state is instance-local;
- evidence is attributable to the instance and exact capability/function version;
- fault containment is explicit;
- cross-instance messages are typed, versioned, validated and policy-checked;
- permissions are evaluated for the actor, instance, task, resource and action;
- no permission or credential is implicitly inherited from another instance.

The 99 lineage slots remain the approved structural capacity: 9 UMIC-domain lineages, 9 Master Core lineages and 81 Engine lineages. This document does not assign the listed identities to those slots. No slot mapping may be inferred from naming, symmetry, historical counts or convenience.

## 6. Capability lifecycle and activation gates

Every candidate follows the canonical lifecycle:

**Discovered → Proposed → Under Evaluation → Approved → Integrated / Rejected / Retired**

Activation is a separate operational readiness decision within the lifecycle and requires, at minimum:
- confirmed identity, owner and responsibility;
- source provenance, version and licence review;
- defined input/output contract and compatibility;
- security classification and least-privilege permission scope;
- resource, latency, reliability and failure-mode assessment;
- tests and evidence appropriate to capability risk;
- dependency and conflict analysis;
- rollback, disablement and recovery plan;
- independent review where risk requires it.

A discovered, proposed, dormant, quarantined or unverified capability is preserved and evaluated; it is not silently executed. Rejected, deferred, superseded and retired capabilities retain their history and reconsideration rationale.

- destination lineage ID and UMIC assignment;

The historical 6+3 Master Core variant, 27-Engine/3-AFI variant, 81 historical Sub-Core variant and candidate nine-domain taxonomies remain reconciliation leads only. They must not be silently equated with the current 9+9+81 lineage structure.

The table in Section 3 is therefore an identity-reconciliation list, not a completed 99-lineage mapping.

## 8. Validation and acceptance

The integration is not complete until tests demonstrate:
1. one canonical AFI PRIME function identity and exactly nine scoped instances;
2. no additional top-level fusion authority or tenth instance;
3. traceable capability identity, provenance, version and lifecycle;
4. compatible specialist contracts and safe rejection of malformed inputs;
5. dependency-aware scheduling without violating ordering constraints;
6. bounded resource use, cancellation, timeout and backpressure;
7. preservation of conflicting findings and uncertainty;
8. denial of unauthorised cross-instance access and permission inheritance;
9. fault containment and recovery without silent state loss;
10. independent verification of consequential outcomes;
11. safe integration, disablement, rollback, rejection and retirement;
12. exact-head CI and independent architecture/security review before promotion.

Documentation or a successful structural test alone does not prove operational capability. Runtime activation requires implementation evidence.
